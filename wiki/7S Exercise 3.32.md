**Exercise 3.32.** A monoid in which every morphism is an isomorphism is a [[Group]]. Is $\mathbb{N}$ (Example 3.13) a group? Is $\mathcal{C}$ from Example 3.18 ($s \mathbin{;} s = z$)?

## Solution

$\mathbb{N}$ is not: $s$ has no inverse, since $s^n \mathbin{;} s = s^{n+1} \neq s^0$. $\mathcal{C}$ is: $s$ is its own inverse; it is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
