The **pushout** of a [[Span]] $X \xleftarrow{g} Z \xrightarrow{f} Y$ is its [[Colimit]]: an object $X +_Z Y$ with maps $\iota_X : X \to X +_Z Y$, $\iota_Y : Y \to X +_Z Y$ (and $\iota_Z = \iota_X \circ g = \iota_Y \circ f$) making the square commute, such that for any $W$ with $\iota'_X, \iota'_Y$ commuting there is a unique $p_W : X +_Z Y \to W$ with $p_W \circ \iota_X = \iota'_X$, $p_W \circ \iota_Y = \iota'_Y$. "$X +_Z Y$ is the [[Coproduct]] of $X$ and $Y$ but with the image of $Z$ in $X$ *equalized by fiat* with the image of $Z$ in $Y$."

## In $\mathbf{Set}$ (Kittenlab)

$X +_Z Y = (X + Y)/\!\sim$ where $\sim$ is the [[Equivalence Relation]] generated by $f(z) \sim g(z)$ for all $z \in Z$. The universal property holds because "by the commutation property, all elements of each equivalence class have to go to the same element of $W$". Implementation: put $X$ and $Y$ side by side in a **union-find** on $n + m$ elements, `union!` the pairs $(g(z), n + f(z))$, and read off roots. The same works for [[Category of Graphs|graphs]] and any [[C-Set]]: glue vertices and edges separately — "take two graphs, take their coproduct, and glue some of their edges and vertices together according to maps out of a third graph".

## As a representable

Let $\mathsf{D}$ be the category $\bullet \leftarrow \bullet \to \bullet$. The pushout of $F : \mathsf{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ is a [[Representable Functor|representing object]] for $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{D}}}(F, \Delta(-))$: a natural transformation $F \Rightarrow \Delta(W)$ is three morphisms, and naturality is exactly the commuting square; feeding the identity of the representing object into the isomorphism gives the cocone $\iota_X, \iota_Y, \iota_Z$, and naturality gives the factorization property (Kittenlab Lecture 9). Since isomorphic diagrams have isomorphic representing objects, pushouts are well defined on isomorphism classes of spans — used to show that composition of [[Cospan|cospans]] is well defined (Lecture 15).

## Uses

- Composition in the [[Cospan|cospan category]] $\mathrm{Csp}(\mathcal{C})$ and in [[Hypergraph Category|hypergraph categories]] / [[Decorated Cospan|decorated cospans]] (7 Sketches Chapter 6: gluing circuits along shared terminals); [[Open Graph|open graphs]].
- Pushing forward a [[Partition]] along a function (7 Sketches §1.4.2).
- Any finite colimit is built from coproducts and pushouts / coequalizers (7 Sketches §6.2.4). Dual: [[Pullback]].

# Kittenlab Lecture 9: pushout of finite sets via union-find
struct UnionFind; parent::Vector{Int}; UnionFind(n) = new(collect(1:n)); end
find_root(uf, i) = uf.parent[i] == i ? i : find_root(uf, uf.parent[i])
union!(uf, i, j) = (uf.parent[find_root(uf, j)] = find_root(uf, i))
function pushout_sets(f::Vector{Int}, g::Vector{Int}, n::Int, m::Int)   # f: Z → {1..n}, g: Z → {1..m}
  po = UnionFind(n + m)
  for z in eachindex(f); union!(po, f[z], n + g[z]); end
  roots = unique!([find_root(po, i) for i in 1:(n + m)])
  (Set(roots), Dict(i => find_root(po, i) for i in 1:n), Dict(j => find_root(po, n + j) for j in 1:m))
end
pushout_sets([1, 2], [1, 1], 3, 2)      # glue x1~y1, x2~y1: three classes
