#definition #example

In a [[Preorder]] $(P, \leq)$, elements $x, y$ are **equivalent**, written $x \cong y$, if $x \leq y$ and $y \leq x$. This is an [[Equivalence Relation]] on $P$ and therefore induces a [[Partition]] of $P$ (Example 1.49) — "$A$ induces $B$" meaning we have an automatic way to turn an $A$ into a $B$.

> Sources: 7 Sketches Definition 1.30, Example 1.49, Remark 1.35, 1.82.

A [[Partial Order]] is a preorder in which $x \cong y$ implies $x = y$; the quotient $P/\!\cong$ is always a partial order, the [[Skeleton]] of $P$ viewed as a category. Elements defined by universal properties — e.g. two [[Meet|meets]] of the same subset — are automatically equivalent (Remark 1.82), which is why we may write "the" meet: "any two things defined by the same universal property are unique up to unique isomorphism". In a category, the analogue of $\cong$ is [[Isomorphism]].
