#example

The **real numbers** $\mathbb{R}$ form a [[Preorder]] with the "usual ordering", e.g. $-500 \leq -499 \leq 0 \leq \sqrt{2} \leq 100/3$. It is a [[Total Order]] ([[7S Chapter 1 Exercises#Exercise 1.48|7S Exercise 1.48]]) and a [[Partial Order]].

> Sources: 7 Sketches Example 1.47, 1.97, §1.3.1; Kittenlab Lecture 5, 7, 10, 11.

- The subset $\mathbb{N} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ has a [[Meet]] ($0$) but no [[Join]]; $\{\frac{1}{n+1} \mid n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ has meet $0$ and join $1$ ([[7S Chapter 1 Exercises#Exercise 1.80|7S Exercise 1.80]]).
- The maps $\lceil -/3 \rceil \dashv (3 \times -) \dashv \lfloor -/3 \rfloor$ between $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{R}$ form [[Galois Connection|Galois connections]] (Example 1.97, [[7S Chapter 1 Exercises#Exercise 1.98|7S Exercise 1.98]]).
- [[7S Chapter 1 Exercises#Exercise 1.1|7S Exercise 1.1]] distinguishes order-, metric-, and addition-preserving functions $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.
- Kittenlab: order-preserving maps $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ form a poset under pointwise order, the [[Natural Transformation|natural transformations]] between them (Lecture 7); the functor $U : \mathbf{Vect} \to \mathbf{Set}$ is [[Representable Functor|represented]] by $\mathbb{R}$ (Lecture 10); the [[Coequalizer]] of $\mathrm{id}, (+2\pi) : \mathbb{R} \rightrightarrows \mathbb{R}$ is the circle (Lecture 11); in $(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}, \leq)$ the functor $[2 \leq x^2]$ is not representable but in $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ it is, represented by $\sqrt 2$.
- $[0, \infty]$ with the *reverse* order is the base of enrichment for [[Lawvere Metric Space|Lawvere metric spaces]] ([[Cost]]).
