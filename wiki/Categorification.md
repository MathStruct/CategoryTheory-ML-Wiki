**Categorification** takes a known structure and adds structure so that what were *properties* become *structures*, in such a way that the original is recovered by forgetting the new structure. "This is rather vague; let's give an example."

> Sources: 7 Sketches §4.4.1–4.4.2 (Remark 4.46–4.47, 4.53), §4.6; [BD98; CY96].

- **$\mathbb{N}$ to $\mathbf{FinSet}$**: replace $5, 3, 8$ by five-, three- and eight-element sets, $+$ by disjoint union $\sqcup$, and the *property* $5 + 3 = 8$ by the *structure* of an isomorphism $\underline{5} \sqcup \underline{3} \cong \underline{8}$; the analogue of the equation is still true, and "we can be more precise about how things relate". Choosing a good categorification "is part of the art of mathematics… its success is often empirical".
- **Preorders to categories**: the brute property "there exists a morphism $a \to b$" ($P(a, b) = \mathsf{true}$) becomes "here is a *set* of morphisms $a \to b$" — hom-Booleans become hom-sets; preorders are $\mathbf{Bool}$-categories and categories are $\mathbf{Set}$-categories ([[Enriched Category]]).
- **[[Wiring Diagram|Wiring diagrams]]** (§4.4.2): for preorders, boxes are anonymous $\leq$; for categories the boxes carry morphism *names*, and the coherence laws (associativity, unitality) are "precisely what is needed to lengthen and shorten wires without ambiguity": $f \mathbin{;} (g \mathbin{;} h) = (f \mathbin{;} g) \mathbin{;} h$ means the composite box is unambiguous, and identities may be inserted or discarded. Combining with the multi-port boxes of [[Symmetric Monoidal Preorder|monoidal preorders]] gives [[Symmetric Monoidal Category|symmetric monoidal categories]].
- **Monoidal preorders to monoidal categories**: the equation $(p \otimes q) \otimes r = p \otimes (q \otimes r)$ becomes the associator isomorphism $\alpha_{s,t,u} : (S \times T) \times U \cong S \times (T \times U)$ — "just a matter of bookkeeping" — but "new brute stuff emerges, and tends to be more complex": the $\alpha$'s must satisfy coherence conditions (pentagon, triangle). "The only way out of this morass is to add infinitely much structure, which leads to $\infty$-categories." Mac Lane's coherence theorem lets us pretend monoidal categories are strict (Remark 4.46–4.47).
- **$\mathcal{V}$-categories** (Remark 4.53): the *properties* $I \leq \mathcal{X}(x,x)$ and $\mathcal{X}(x,y) \otimes \mathcal{X}(y,z) \leq \mathcal{X}(x,z)$ become *structures*: chosen identity elements $\mathrm{id}_x : I \to \mathcal{X}(x,x)$ and composition morphisms. In a preorder "we do not need to choose, we just need to make sure they exist".
- **Categories to bicategories**: composition associative only up to isomorphism ([[Category of Profunctors|profunctors]], [[Cospan|cospans]]); symmetric monoidal bicategories categorify symmetric monoidal categories (§4.6). Kittenlab Lecture 15: "we choose not to walk that route today".
