#definition #example

The **interval domain** $\mathbb{I}\mathbb{R}$ is the [[Topological Space]] whose points are the finite closed intervals

$$
\mathbb{I}\mathbb{R} := \{[d, u] \subseteq \mathbb{R} \mid d \leq u\},
$$

with topology generated by the **basic open sets** $o_{[a,b]} := \{[d, u] \in \mathbb{I}\mathbb{R} \mid a < d \leq u < b\}$ for $a < b$: a subset is open iff it is a union of basic opens. Sheaves on $\mathbb{I}\mathbb{R}$ are the [[Topos of Behavior Types|behavior types]].

> Sources: 7 Sketches §7.5.1, Exercises 7.76, 7.77; §7.5.2; §7.6 (domain theory, [Gie+03], [AJ94]).

- $o_{[0,5]}$ contains every interval inside $(0, 5)$. Note $o_{[0,5]} \cup o_{[4,8]} \neq o_{[0,8]}$: the interval $[2, 6]$ lies in the right-hand side but in neither piece of the left ([[7S Exercise 7.76]]). This is exactly why $\mathbb{I}\mathbb{R}$, not $\mathbb{R}$, is the right site for statements that *take time to evaluate*: "the stock market did not drop more than 10 points" can be true on $(0,5)$ and on $(4,8)$ without being true on $(0,8)$.
- $\mathbb{R}$ embeds as the subspace $\{[d, u] \mid d = u\}$, and the subspace topology agrees with the usual one on $\mathbb{R}$ ([[7S Exercise 7.77]]): $o_{[a,b]} \cap \mathbb{R} = (a, b)$.
- A sheaf $S$ on $\mathbb{I}\mathbb{R}$ is determined by its values on basic opens, and these vary continuously: $S(o_{[a,b]}) \cong \lim_{\epsilon > 0} S(o_{[a - \epsilon, b + \epsilon]})$.
- The name comes from domain theory (Dana Scott): intervals ordered by reverse inclusion form a continuous domain whose Scott topology this is.

# points of 𝕀ℝ as pairs (d, u), basic opens as predicates
struct Interval; d::Float64; u::Float64; end
basic(a, b) = I -> a < I.d <= I.u < b        # o_[a,b]
I = Interval(2, 6)
basic(0, 8)(I)                               # true
basic(0, 5)(I) || basic(4, 8)(I)             # false: o_[0,5] ∪ o_[4,8] ≠ o_[0,8]
