The **coequalizer** of a parallel pair $p, q : A \rightrightarrows B$ is the [[Colimit]] of the diagram $\bullet \rightrightarrows \bullet$: an object $C$ with a **coequalizing morphism** $e : B \to C$ such that $e \circ p = e \circ q$, universal: any $f : B \to X$ with $f \circ p = f \circ q$ factors uniquely as $f = \tilde{f} \circ e$.

```tikz

\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r, "p", shift left] \arrow[r, "q"', shift right] & B \arrow[r, "e"] \arrow[dr, "f"'] & C \arrow[d, "\tilde f", dashed] \\
 & & X
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```

Kittenlab's definition: the coequalizer is a [[Representable Functor|representing object]] for $F(X) = \{f : B \to X \mid f \circ p = f \circ q\}$; the proposition that a representative comes with a coequalizing morphism $e$ (corresponding to $\mathrm{id}_C \in y_C(C)$) and conversely, is "much less data to lug around than a whole natural isomorphism". "If [[Coproduct|coproducts]] allow you to *add* objects, coequalizers allow you to *squish* them."

> Sources: Kittenlab Lecture 9 (quotient group $G/H$), 11 ("Adding and squishing": angles); DaoFP §9.5 ("Coequalizers"), Exercise 9.5.2; 7 Sketches §6.2.4 (finite colimits in $\mathbf{Set}$), §1.2.1 ([[Quotient Set]]); CTfS Definition 2.6.3.1, Exercises 2.6.3.2–2.6.3.3, 3.1.2.4, 3.3.1.10

## Examples

- **Angles** (Kittenlab): a program taking an angle should agree on $\theta$ and $\theta + 2\pi$; the functor $F(X) = \{f : \mathbb{R} \to X \mid f \circ 1_{\mathbb{R}} = f \circ (+2\pi)\}$ (functions of period $2\pi$) is represented by $[0, 2\pi)$, by $[-\pi, \pi)$ (convenient for pendulums), and by the circle $S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$ with $e(\theta) = e^{i\theta}$. In $\mathbf{Set}$ all three are isomorphic; in $\mathbf{Top}$ only $S^1$ works, since $\mathbb{R} \to [0, 2\pi)$ "jumps" — the coequalizer depends on the category.
- **In $\mathbf{Set}$**: $C = B/\!\sim$ where $\sim$ is the [[Equivalence Relation]] generated by $p(a) \sim q(a)$; "$C$ is a copy of $B$ in which elements produced from the same $a$ have been identified" — *bucketizing* (DaoFP): coequalizing $\mathsf{fst}, \mathsf{snd}$ on pairs of integers of equal parity gives `Bool` with `q n = even n`, and any parity-only `q'` factors as `h q' . q` ([[DaoFP Chapter 9 Exercises#Exercise 9.5.2|DaoFP Exercise 9.5.2]]).
- **The clock** (CTfS Exercises 2.6.3.2, 3.1.2.4): coequalizing $\mathrm{id}, (x \mapsto x + 1) : \mathbb{R} \rightrightarrows \mathbb{R}$ gives the circle $[0, 1)$; with $x \mapsto x + 12$ it gives the clock face $T = [0, 12)$, and the universal property is exactly what makes "advance the clock by $x$ hours" a well-defined map $T \to T$ — an action of the monoid $(\mathbb{R}, 0, +)$ on the clock ([[Monoid Action]]).
- **Graphs** (CTfS Exercise 3.3.1.10): a graph is a parallel pair $\mathrm{src}, \mathrm{tgt} : A \rightrightarrows V$; its coequalizer is the set of connected components (its [[Equalizer]] is the set of loops).
- **Quotient groups** (Kittenlab Lecture 9): in $\mathbf{Ab}$, the coequalizer of the inclusion $H \hookrightarrow G$ and the zero map is $G/H$ — "declaring by fiat every element of $H$ to be $0$".
- **Matrices** (Kittenlab Lecture 11): in $\mathbf{Mat}$, the coequalizer of $M, 0 : n \rightrightarrows m$ is $m - \mathrm{rank}(M)$, with coequalizing matrix $E$ whose rows are an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of the range of $M$: $EM = 0$, and any $E'$ with $E'M = 0$ factors through $E$.
- A [[Pushout]] is a coequalizer of the two injections into a coproduct; every [[Colimit]] is a coequalizer of coproducts.

````tabs
# Kittenlab Lecture 11: the coequalizer of M and 0 in Mat via an orthonormal complement
using LinearAlgebra
function coequalizer_mat(M)
  m, k = size(M, 1), rank(M)
  Q = Matrix(qr(hcat(M, Matrix{Float64}(I, m, m))).Q)   # extend an orthonormal basis of range(M)
  Q[:, k+1:m]'                                          # rows v_{k+1}..v_m
end
M = [1.0 0; 0 0; 1 0]; E = coequalizer_mat(M); norm(E * M) < 1e-12   # true
```
````
