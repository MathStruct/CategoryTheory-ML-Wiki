## Solution 5.1.1.3

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.1.1.3|Exercise 5.1.1.3]]

$\beta(f)$ is the unique monoid homomorphism extending $f$: it sends a list to the product of the images of its entries. So $\beta(f)([b, b, a, c]) = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 7 \cdot 2 = 56$ (and $\beta(f)([\,]) = 1$) ([[Free-Forgetful Adjunction]], [[Free Monoid]]).

## Solution 5.1.1.6

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.1.1.6|Exercise 5.1.1.6]]

- **Left adjoint** $\mathrm{Disc}$: a set $S$ ↦ the discrete graph with vertex set $S$ and no arrows. A graph map $\mathrm{Disc}(S) \to G$ is just a function $S \to V_G$, since there are no arrows to preserve.
- **Right adjoint** $\mathrm{Cod}$: $S$ ↦ the "complete" graph with vertices $S$ and exactly one arrow $s \to s'$ for every ordered pair, including a loop at each vertex, i.e. arrows $S \times S$ with $\mathrm{src} = \pi_1$, $\mathrm{tgt} = \pi_2$. A graph map $G \to \mathrm{Cod}(S)$ is just a function $V_G \to S$, because every arrow has a unique possible image.

So $\mathrm{Disc} \dashv U \dashv \mathrm{Cod}$ ([[Adjunction]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.1.1.6; see [[Adjunction]], [[Map of Content]].

## Solution 5.1.1.9

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.1.1.9|Exercise 5.1.1.9]]

$p = \pi_0$, the set of *connected components*: objects of $\mathcal C$ modulo the equivalence relation generated by "there is a morphism $c \to c'$". A functor $\mathcal C \to \mathrm{Disc}(S)$ must send every morphism to an identity, so it is constant on connected components, i.e. a function $\pi_0(\mathcal C) \to S$. Hint check: functors $[1] \to \mathrm{Disc}(\underline 2)$ are the 2 constant ones, and functions $\pi_0([1]) = \{\ast\} \to \underline 2$ are 2 ✓ (the set of objects would give 4). So $\pi_0 \dashv \mathrm{Disc} \dashv \mathrm{Ob} \dashv \mathrm{Ind}$ ([[Adjunction]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.1.1.9; see [[Adjunction]], [[Map of Content]].

## Solution 5.2.3.3

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.2.3.3|Exercise 5.2.3.3]]

- **a.** Take the four open quadrant-ish squares $U_1 = [0, 2) \times [0, 2)$, $U_2 = (1, 3] \times [0, 2)$, $U_3 = [0, 2) \times (1, 3]$, $U_4 = (1, 3] \times (1, 3]$ (open in $X$). The pairwise overlaps are $U_{12}, U_{13}, U_{24}, U_{34}$ (strips) and $U_{14}, U_{23}$. All the triple overlaps and the 4-fold overlap equal the central square $(1, 2) \times (1, 2)$. The preorder has arrows from each overlap to the sets it lies in. Temperature bounds: $R_1(x, y) = 10 + x$ and $R_2(x, y) = 20 + x + y$.
- **b.** For $B \subseteq A$ the restriction $\mathcal O(A) \to \mathcal O(B)$ sends $f \mapsto f|_B$; the bounds still hold on $B$. "Value-assignment throughout $A$" is apt: an element is a choice of temperature at every point of $A$, within the bounds. It even satisfies the sheaf condition, because compatible local assignments glue uniquely.
- **c.** Yes: the inclusions $\mathcal O'(A) \hookrightarrow \mathcal O(A)$ commute with restriction (the restriction of a continuous function is continuous), so they form a natural transformation. $\mathcal O'$ is a sub-presheaf, in fact a sub-*sheaf*, since continuity is local. See [[Sheaf]], [[Presheaf]].

## Solution 5.3.2.5

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.3.2.5|Exercise 5.3.2.5]]

$\eta_X : X \to X \sqcup E$ is the coproduct inclusion, meaning "return a value, no exception". $\mu_X : (X \sqcup E) \sqcup E \to X \sqcup E$ is the identity on $X$ and on the inner copy of $E$, and sends the outer copy of $E$ to $E$ identically. Both copies of the exceptions merge: an exception raised at either stage is reported. The unit laws hold because $\mu \circ \eta_{TX}$ and $\mu \circ T\eta_X$ are both the identity of $X \sqcup E$. Associativity holds because both ways of flattening $((X \sqcup E) \sqcup E) \sqcup E$ merge all three copies of $E$. This is Haskell's `Either e`. Kleisli composition short-circuits on the first exception ([[Maybe Monad]], [[Monad]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.3.2.5; see [[Map of Content]], [[Maybe Monad]].

## Solution 5.3.3.5

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.3.3.5|Exercise 5.3.3.5]]

- **a.** $R_f = \{(a, b) \mid b \in f(a)\}$. Conversely a relation $R$ gives $f_R(a) = \{b \mid (a, b) \in R\}$. These are inverse bijections $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Kls}}(A, B) \cong \mathcal P(A \times B)$.
- **b.** They agree: $(g \circ_{\mathcal P} f)(a) = \bigcup_{b \in f(a)} g(b)$, so $(a, c) \in R_{g \circ f}$ iff there exists $b$ with $(a, b) \in R_f$ and $(b, c) \in R_g$, the relational composite. The identities $\eta$ are the diagonal relations. Hence $\mathrm{Kls}(\mathcal P) \cong \mathbf{Rel}$ ([[Power Set Monad]], [[Category of Relations]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.3.3.5; see [[Map of Content]], [[Power Set Monad]].

## Solution 5.3.3.6

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.3.3.6|Exercise 5.3.3.6]]

The disjoint union $A \sqcup B = \{1, 2, 3, a, b\}$, *not* the Cartesian product. The projections are the relations $A \sqcup B \to A$ and $A \sqcup B \to B$ that are the identity on the matching summand and empty on the other. A pair of relations $R \subseteq Z \times A$, $S \subseteq Z \times B$ corresponds to exactly one relation $Z \to A \sqcup B$, namely their union. Since $\mathbf{Rel} \cong \mathbf{Rel}^{\mathrm{op}}$ via transposition, this is the dual of the coproduct statement ([[Power Set Monad]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.3.3.6; see [[Map of Content]], [[Power Set Monad]].

## Solution 5.3.3.7

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.3.3.7|Exercise 5.3.3.7]]

Also $A \sqcup B = \{1, 2, 3, a, b\}$, with the inclusions (as relations) as injections. A relation out of $A \sqcup B$ is a pair of relations out of $A$ and out of $B$. Products and coproducts coincide, so $\mathbf{Rel}$ has *biproducts*, as matrices over the Booleans do ([[Power Set Monad]], [[Coproduct]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.3.3.7; see [[Map of Content]], [[Power Set Monad]].

## Solution 5.4.1.4

[[CTfS Chapter 5 Exercises#Exercise 5.4.1.4|Exercise 5.4.1.4]]

- **a.** $f : (\text{circle}, \text{circle}, \text{square}) \to \text{triangle}$: inside a big triangle, place two small disjoint circles near the bottom corners and a small square near the top vertex, none overlapping.
- **b.** One way to factor it is $f = g \circ (h, \mathrm{id}_{\text{square}})$ with the following pieces:
  - $h : (\text{circle}, \text{circle}) \to \text{square}$ places two small circles side by side in a square;
  - $\mathrm{id}_{\text{square}}$;
  - $g : (\text{square}, \text{square}) \to \text{triangle}$ places a wide square along the bottom and a small square at the top.

  Composition substitutes $h$ into the first square of $g$ (after rescaling), which puts the two circles in the bottom region of the triangle and leaves the top square alone. That is a positioning of the shape of $f$. So the three morphisms $g$, $h$ and $\mathrm{id}_{\text{square}}$ compose to $f$. Composition is associative because nested substitution is ([[Operad]]).

> Sources: CTfS, Exercise 5.4.1.4; see [[Map of Content]], [[Operad]].
