#definition #example

A **finite presentation** of a category is a finite [[Graph]] together with **path equations**: equations $p = q$ between *parallel* paths (same source vertex and same target vertex). The resulting category — [[Free Category|paths]] modulo the equations and their consequences — is a **finitely presented category**. A [[Database Schema]] is exactly such a presentation.

> Sources: 7 Sketches §3.2.2, Examples 3.18, 3.38, 3.41, Exercises 3.16, 3.17, 3.19, 3.21, Remark 3.20; Kittenlab Lecture 6 ("finitely-presented categories, i.e. categories generated by a graph"); Catlab `@present`.

## Examples

**Free square vs. commutative square.**

```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r, "f"] \arrow[d, "g"'] & B \arrow[d, "h"] & & A \arrow[r, "f"] \arrow[d, "g"'] & B \arrow[d, "h"] \\
C \arrow[r, "i"'] & D & & C \arrow[r, "i"'] & D
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```

Left, no equations: ten morphisms $A, A\mathbin{;}f, A\mathbin{;}g, A\mathbin{;}f\mathbin{;}h, A\mathbin{;}g\mathbin{;}i, B, B\mathbin{;}h, C, C\mathbin{;}i, D$ (identities named by their objects; [[7S Exercise 3.16]]). Right, with $f \mathbin{;} h = g \mathbin{;} i$: nine morphisms — "the missing one is $g \mathbin{;} i$" is not quite right, it is there too, equal to $f \mathbin{;} h$. Adding a diagonal $j : A \to D$ with $f \mathbin{;} h = j = g \mathbin{;} i$ still gives nine ([[7S Exercise 3.17]]).

**Equations imply more equations** (Example 3.18): with one vertex $z$, one loop $s$ and $s \mathbin{;} s = z$, we get $s \mathbin{;} s \mathbin{;} s = s$ etc.; the category is $\{z, s\}$ with $s \mathbin{;} s = z$ — the [[Group]] $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. With $s^4 = s^2$ instead, there are four morphisms $z, s, s^2, s^3$ ([[7S Exercise 3.19]]).

**Preorders as presented categories.** A [[Hasse Diagram]] presents a preorder: all parallel paths are equated (§3.2.3, [[7S Exercise 3.21]]); e.g. the partition lattice Eq. (1.5) needs $a\mathbin{;}d = b\mathbin{;}e = c\mathbin{;}f$. Every presentation lies between the free category and the [[Preorder Reflection]].

**Functors out of presented categories** (Example 3.38, 3.41): there is exactly one functor from the free square $\mathcal{F}$ to the commutative square $\mathcal{C}$ matching the objects — the equations are *satisfied*; but there is none $\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{F}$ matching objects, because functors preserve equations: $f' \mathbin{;} h' = F(f \mathbin{;} h) = F(g \mathbin{;} i) = g' \mathbin{;} i'$ would have to hold in the free category. Functors preserving composition is exactly what enforces database *business rules*.

# Catlab: presentations with path equations
using Catlab
@present CommSquare(FreeCategory) begin
  (A, B, C, D)::Ob
  f::Hom(A, B); g::Hom(A, C); h::Hom(B, D); i::Hom(C, D)
  compose(f, h) == compose(g, i)
end
equations(CommSquare)          # the one path equation

@present Z2(FreeCategory) begin
  z::Ob
  s::Hom(z, z)
  compose(s, s) == id(z)
end

# 7 Sketches' mySchema, with the two business rules
@present MySchema(FreeSchema) begin
  (Employee, Department)::Ob
  Mngr::Hom(Employee, Employee); WorksIn::Hom(Employee, Department); Secr::Hom(Department, Employee)
  String::AttrType
  FName::Attr(Employee, String); DName::Attr(Department, String)
  compose(Secr, WorksIn) == id(Department)
  compose(Mngr, WorksIn) == WorksIn
end
