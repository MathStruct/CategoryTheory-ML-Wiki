**Definition 6.30.** A category $\mathcal{C}$ **has finite colimits** if $\mathrm{colim}_{\mathcal{J}} D$ exists for every finite category $\mathcal{J}$ (finitely many morphisms, hence objects) and diagram $D : \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}$.

**Proposition 6.32.** The following are equivalent: (1) $\mathcal{C}$ has all finite colimits; (2) $\mathcal{C}$ has an [[Initial Object]] and all [[Pushout|pushouts]]; (3) $\mathcal{C}$ has all [[Coequalizer|coequalizers]] and finite [[Coproduct|coproducts]]. (Proof idea: build any finite diagram inductively from these building blocks — e.g. the colimit of $A \to B \to Z$, $A \to C$, $A \to D$... is a pushout of pushouts, Example 6.33, [[7S Exercise 6.35]]; [Bor94, Prop 2.8.2].)

**Corollary 6.36.** $\mathbf{FinSet}$ and $\mathbf{Set}$ have all finite colimits.

**Theorem 6.37 (formula).** Let $\mathcal{J}$ be presented by a finite graph $(V, A, s, t)$ with equations and $D : \mathcal{J} \to \mathbf{Set}$. Then
$$\mathrm{colim}_{\mathcal{J}} D := \{(v, d) \mid v \in V,\ d \in D(v)\} / \!\sim$$
where $\sim$ is the [[Equivalence Relation]] generated by $(v, d) \sim (w, e)$ whenever there is $a : v \to w$ with $D(a)(d) = e$, together with $\iota_v : D(v) \to \mathrm{colim}\,D$ sending $d$ to its class, is a colimit of $D$. "Whereas a finite limit is a subset of a product, a finite colimit is a quotient of a coproduct" — dual to [[Finite Limits in Set]].

> Sources: 7 Sketches §6.2.4 (Definition 6.30, Examples 6.31, 6.33, 6.38–6.42, Proposition 6.32, Corollary 6.36, Theorem 6.37, Exercises 6.35, 6.41); Kittenlab Lecture 9.

**Instances.** Empty graph: $\varnothing$, the initial object (Example 6.38). Two vertices: $X \sqcup Y$ (Example 6.39). One vertex: $D(v)$ itself (Example 6.40). Span $X \xleftarrow{f} N \xrightarrow{g} Y$: $(X \sqcup Y)/(f(n) \sim g(n))$, the pushout ([[7S Exercise 6.41]]; the copy of $N$ is redundant since every $n$ is identified with $f(n)$). Parallel pair $f, g : X \rightrightarrows Y$: $Y / (f(x) \sim g(x))$, the coequalizer (Example 6.42) — the terminals of an interconnected circuit. Example 6.29: with $f(a) = \lfloor a/2 \rfloor$, $g(a) = \lfloor (a+1)/2 \rfloor$ on $\mathbb{N}$, the pushout is a single point, since $0 \sim 0' \sim 1 \sim 1' \sim \cdots$ by induction.

# Theorem 6.37 with union-find: colimit of a finite diagram in FinSet
using DataStructures
function set_colimit(sets::Vector{Int}, arrows::Vector{Tuple{Int,Int,Vector{Int}}})   # (v, w, D(a))
  offs = cumsum([0; sets[1:end-1]])                 # (v, d) ↦ offs[v] + d in the disjoint union
  uf = IntDisjointSets(sum(sets))
  for (v, w, Da) in arrows, d in 1:sets[v]
    union!(uf, offs[v] + d, offs[w] + Da[d])
  end
  roots = unique([find_root!(uf, i) for i in 1:sum(sets)])
  (length(roots), [Dict(d => findfirst(==(find_root!(uf, offs[v] + d)), roots) for d in 1:sets[v]) for v in eachindex(sets)])
end
